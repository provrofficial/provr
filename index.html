The problem

AI is writing the chart. Nobody can see where it goes wrong.

Hospitals now use AI tools to write notes, summarize charts, draft messages, and suggest next steps. When one tool drops an allergy or changes a dose, the next tool inherits the mistake. Finding where it started means reading each system by hand, after the fact.

Meet Maria, a synthetic patient. She is 58 and has been allergic to penicillin since childhood. Three AI tools now write about her chart.

Why it matters

Behind every chart is a person, and a family.

When an AI tool drops an allergy or changes a dose, the people who pay for it are a parent, a grandparent, a child. Provr exists so the people who care for them can trust what the record says.

  • Messages to patients and families are designed to be checked against the source
  • Care teams see where a mistake started and fix it there
  • Safety teams get evidence, not guesses

Our approach

An independent check on every AI in the chart.

A tool that grades its own work is a weak safety story. Provr is designed to sit beside your EHR and your AI vendors, not inside any of them.

Works across vendors

Follows one patient fact through tools from different companies, including AI built into your own EHR.

Independent record

A tamper-evident log held outside the systems it checks, so you can show a board or a regulator who checked what.

Watches every update

Re-checks a test set after each software update and flags new errors before they reach patients.

These describe our design goals. Early pilots will show how well they hold up.

Questions

What hospital teams ask first.

Who is Provr for?

Everyone who relies on AI-written documentation: surgeons, nurses, physical therapists, pharmacists, radiology and lab teams, primary care, and emergency and inpatient teams. Safety and quality leaders use it to find the root cause across all of them. A pilot would start with one workflow.

Does Provr change the patient's chart?

No. Provr is designed to observe. It never edits the record, and doubtful cases go to a person for review.

Does it make clinical decisions?

No. It flags mismatches between a source and an AI-written document for a person to review. It does not diagnose or choose treatments.

Will staff have to do extra work?

That is the design goal. Provr is meant to listen to results and documents that hospital systems already send to each other, so clinicians upload and enter nothing. Setup is done once by the hospital's IT team, and it needs their review and approval.

What data would a pilot use?

We would start with synthetic or de-identified data, under your privacy and security process. Every demo on this site uses made-up patients.

Is this ready for production?

No. Provr is early stage. The screens here are a working demo built on synthetic patients, and the checks cover a limited set of cases today. We are looking for a partner to shape and test it with.

Want to see where your AI could go wrong?

We are looking for hospital safety, quality, and informatics teams to give honest feedback and help shape a small pilot.
